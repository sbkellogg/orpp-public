Advanced Teaching Roles Class Size Report

North Carolina Department of Public Instruction

Educator workforceWorking ConditionsData systems

What the study found

The report proposes team-level student-to-teacher ratios that account for leadership release time. A small, one-year Classroom Excellence analysis found no clear class-size relationship with teacher growth scores.

Considerations for practice

Capture team membership and release time when describing staffing arrangements.

Limitations and interpretation

A very small observational sample and one year of data cannot establish that larger classes are harmless. Recommendations and technology plans are not evidence of implementation or effects.

Questions that remain

How do team ratios and coaching time relate to outcomes over multiple years?

Priority 2EmergingRA-010

Staffing measures need to reflect team responsibilities.

Qualifications and staffing

Teacher-assistant and ATR studies address different staffing arrangements. The ATR class-size report proposes team ratios that include release time, but its small one-year analysis cannot establish class-size effects.

Workforce and organizational flexibility

ATR and Restart studies address specific models. The separate ATR staffing report explains team ratios and data needs; it does not establish that larger classes are harmless or that a ratio rule improves outcomes.

Staffing rules require usable linked information.

Policy implementation and administrative burden

The ATR class-size report identifies the need to link teacher leaders, supported teachers, students, and release time for team-level reporting. It offers a feasibility approach, not a tested estimate of reduced burden.
