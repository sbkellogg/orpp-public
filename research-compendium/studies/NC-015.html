The Importance of Teaching and Learning Conditions: Influences on Teacher Retention and School Performance in North Carolina

Barnett Berry; Kevin C. Bastian; Linda Darling-Hammond; Tara Kini

Working ConditionsEducator workforce

What the study found

Leadership, collaboration, planning time, community support, collective practice, and student conduct were positively associated with retention. Associations were often stronger in high-poverty schools.

Considerations for practice

Use working-conditions results to structure local conversations and improvement plans.

Limitations and interpretation

Associations do not establish that changing a survey score causes retention. The study uses an earlier survey and predates the pandemic.

Questions that remain

Do these relationships persist across current survey administrations?

Study at a glance

Design
Mixed methods; observational regression
NC setting & population
2016 NC Teacher Working Conditions survey, administrative records, and 2018–19 teacher interviews and school visits.
