Equal Time for Equal Crime? Racial Bias in School Discipline

Ying Shi; Maria Zhu

Safety & well-being

What the study found

Comparisons within incidents and across incidents involving the same students found Black students faced higher suspension probabilities or longer suspensions than comparison students, consistent with racial bias.

Considerations for practice

Review disciplinary decisions for comparable incidents, rather than interpreting raw group rates alone.

Limitations and interpretation

Administrative data cannot observe every behavioral or contextual difference. The study examines disciplinary decisions, not an evaluated remedy.

Questions that remain

Which changes to decision routines reduce disparities?
