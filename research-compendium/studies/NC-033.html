The Selection and Evaluation of Advanced Teachers: Summative Report

Sarah Bausell; James Birkett; Duncan Culbreth; Brayan Diaz; Shaun Kellogg; Emily Thrasher; Robin Anderson; Lam Pham; Tamara Young; Kari Colley; Sarah Dyson; Caitlin Kearney; Liv Rau; Matthew G. Springer; David Stuit

Educator workforceWorking ConditionsInstructional improvement

What the study found

Districts used varied, multistage selection processes; evaluation practices were less developed and often missed teacher-leadership responsibilities. No single selection measure consistently predicted both student achievement and supported-teacher retention. The study developed the Adult Leadership Teacher Evaluation Rubric (ALTER).

Considerations for practice

Align selection and evaluation with actual leadership duties, include supported-teacher feedback, and pilot ALTER before wider use. Interpret predictive measures alongside local context and professional judgment.

Limitations and interpretation

The predictive analyses use a single-year, nonrepresentative sample with missing data and no independent external validation; associations are not causal effects. ALTER has stakeholder and content review, but its reliability and outcome-related validity still require pilot testing.

Questions that remain

Does ALTER produce reliable, fair ratings across districts, and do selection measures predict outcomes in new cohorts?

Study at a glance

Design
Mixed-methods measurement study; interviews, document review, regression, predictive modeling, and rubric development
NC setting & population
North Carolina ATR programs; 103 stakeholder interviews, 47 program documents, and 106 leader questionnaires, with administrative analyses using 2023–24 outcomes.
Source reviewed
Full report; findings, methods, limitations, and instrument development reviewed
