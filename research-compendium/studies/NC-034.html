Is Read to Achieve Making the Grade? An Assessment of North Carolina’s Elementary Reading Proficiency Initiative

Stephen R. Porter; D. T. Stallings; Sara Tova Pilzer Weiss (listed authors and contributors)

LiteracyInstructional improvement

What the study found

The executive summary reports no statistically detectable reading-score advantage for the first two cohorts, including retained students and reading-camp participants. Implementation varied across districts.

Considerations for practice

Study local delivery and earlier literacy supports when interpreting the historical results.

Limitations and interpretation

The institutional executive summary was reviewed, not the full technical report. Findings concern early implementation before the 2021 literacy reforms and do not evaluate LETRS.

Questions that remain

How have outcomes changed under subsequent literacy reforms?

Priority 3EmergingRA-015

Implementation studies identify barriers that outcome averages can miss.

Implementation

LETRS observations, CCP interviews, and preliminary coaching reports describe how support and access work in practice. Such evidence helps define improvement questions but does not establish that a proposed change causes better outcomes. ATR annual case studies add district-specific evidence about roles and coaching time.

Variation in effects
