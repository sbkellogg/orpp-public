Do Teacher Assistants Improve Student Outcomes? Evidence From School Funding Cutbacks in North Carolina

Steven W. Hemelt; Helen F. Ladd; Calen R. Clifton

Educator workforceSchool financeLiteracy

What the study found

The analysis finds positive teacher-assistant effects on reading and mathematics, with larger benefits for students of color and students in high-poverty schools.

Considerations for practice

Include teacher assistants when considering the mix of staffing investments.

Limitations and interpretation

Historical funding and staffing variation differs from a modern randomized staffing trial. Current costs, duties, and implementation may change the value of an investment.

Questions that remain

Which present-day staffing models produce benefits relative to their costs?
