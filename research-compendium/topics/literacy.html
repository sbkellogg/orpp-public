What we know—and what remains open

Priority 1EmergingRA-002

Early learning benefits depend on the outcome and later environment.

Early learning pathways

NC pre-K studies examine different exposures and follow-up periods. They support looking beyond a single readiness score; results differ across developmental domains and subsequent school contexts.

Priority 2EmergingRA-010

Staffing roles and selection measures address different questions.

Qualifications and staffing

The teacher-assistant study examines a staffing category. The ATR summative report examines teacher selection and measurement: no single selection measure consistently predicted achievement and retention. Its exploratory associations do not establish causal effects of credentials or hiring rules.

Priority 2EmergingRA-011

Professional support shows promise, with uneven outcome evidence.

Professional learning and leadership

The annual ATR evaluation and coaching summary examine support and outcomes. The separate ATR summative study identifies gaps in evaluating teacher leadership and develops ALTER, which still needs pilot testing. These sources offer different evidence; a project description without findings is indexed separately.

Implementation

Variation in effects

Working paper2022 · NC-011

The Long-Term Effect of North Carolina’s Pre-Kindergarten Program Is Larger in School Districts with Lower Rates of Growth in Academic Achievement

Robert C. Carr; Tyler W. Watts; Jade M. Jenkins; Yu Bai; Ellen S. Peisner-Feinberg; Clara G. Muschkin; Helen F. Ladd; Kenneth A. Dodge

Estimated benefits of NC Pre-K funding were larger in districts with slower subsequent achievement growth. Funding benefits also added to the advantages of higher average district achievement.

Journal article2021 · NC-012

Effects of North Carolina’s Pre-Kindergarten Program at the End of Kindergarten: Contributions of School-Wide Quality

Robert C. Carr; Ellen S. Peisner-Feinberg; Rachel Kaplan; Irina L. Mokrova

Average-quality elementary schools showed no reliable overall NC Pre-K effect in this analysis. Language or working-memory benefits were evident under some higher-quality school conditions; literacy and mathematics moderation was not detected.
