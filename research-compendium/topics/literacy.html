What we know—and what remains open

Priority 1EmergingRA-002

Early-learning findings depend on exposure and later context.

Early learning pathways

NC Pre-K studies use both participant and county-funding designs. The added middle-school follow-up reports sustained favorable funding associations; kindergarten findings remain more conditional. Shared cohorts and funding data limit independence across papers.

Priority 2EmergingRA-010

Staffing measures need to reflect team responsibilities.

Qualifications and staffing

Teacher-assistant and ATR studies address different staffing arrangements. The ATR class-size report proposes team ratios that include release time, but its small one-year analysis cannot establish class-size effects.

Priority 2EmergingRA-011

Professional learning needs support for classroom application.

Professional learning and leadership

ATR and coaching sources examine different forms of support. New LETRS observations describe instructional strengths and remaining problems after training, without estimating training effects. ALTER still needs pilot validation; CARES findings remain preliminary.

Priority 3EmergingRA-014

Program results depend on the version and outcome evaluated.

Effectiveness

The collection now includes early Read to Achieve and Opportunity Scholarship evaluations, early-childhood funding research, and ATR and Restart studies. Their populations, policy periods, and designs differ; agency summaries are not independent impact replications.

Priority 3EmergingRA-015

Implementation studies identify barriers that outcome averages can miss.

Implementation

LETRS observations, CCP interviews, and preliminary coaching reports describe how support and access work in practice. Such evidence helps define improvement questions but does not establish that a proposed change causes better outcomes. ATR annual case studies add district-specific evidence about roles and coaching time.

Priority 3EmergingRA-016

Average results can conceal differences across students and settings.

Variation in effects

The indexed early-learning and mathematics studies illustrate variation by context, outcome, or student group. Subgroup findings require careful attention to the underlying design.

Priority 3EmergingRA-017

Effectiveness and affordability need to be examined together.

Cost and return on investment

Teacher-assistant research offers historical staffing evidence. The tutoring project is investigating delivery models, but a complete cost comparison was not reviewed here.

Priority 6EmergingRA-055

Allocation evidence should connect resources, needs, and outcomes.

Resource allocation and school finance

The initial sources cover historical finance analysis, teacher-assistant staffing, and planned ESSER priorities. These measures do not provide a current causal comparison of funding formulas.
