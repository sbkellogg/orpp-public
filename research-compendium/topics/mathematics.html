What we know—and what remains open

Priority 1EmergingRA-002

Early-learning findings depend on exposure and later context.

Early learning pathways

NC Pre-K studies use both participant and county-funding designs. The added middle-school follow-up reports sustained favorable funding associations; kindergarten findings remain more conditional. Shared cohorts and funding data limit independence across papers.

Systematic placement can open later mathematics pathways.

Middle-grades transitions

Wake County’s targeted middle-school acceleration study offers evidence about students near a test-score cutoff. It does not answer every question about grade 8 placement statewide.

Priority 1EmergingRA-004

Eligibility alone does not ensure access.

Advanced learning access

CCP research adds delivery and participation evidence: advising, remaining costs, schedules, and transportation shape opportunities. These qualitative findings complement, rather than establish, effects of changing access rules.

Program results depend on the version and outcome evaluated.

Effectiveness

The collection now includes early Read to Achieve and Opportunity Scholarship evaluations, early-childhood funding research, and ATR and Restart studies. Their populations, policy periods, and designs differ; agency summaries are not independent impact replications.

Priority 3EmergingRA-015

Implementation studies identify barriers that outcome averages can miss.

Implementation

LETRS observations, CCP interviews, and preliminary coaching reports describe how support and access work in practice. Such evidence helps define improvement questions but does not establish that a proposed change causes better outcomes. ATR annual case studies add district-specific evidence about roles and coaching time.

Variation in effects

The indexed early-learning and mathematics studies illustrate variation by context, outcome, or student group. Subgroup findings require careful attention to the underlying design.

Priority 4SubstantialRA-041

Attendance matters, and its causes and remedies differ.

Attendance

NC studies document persistent absence, learning associations, and particular healthcare or policy pathways. A historical charter-entry study adds favorable attendance estimates for that choice context; it does not establish a universal attendance intervention.

Priority 4EmergingRA-046

Recovery needs extend beyond test performance.

Resilience and recovery

Attendance and engagement studies document persistent barriers; tutoring and preliminary CARES findings add specific intervention examples. Agency-reported coaching findings do not establish the overall effects of pandemic relief.

Priority 6EmergingRA-056

Access policies can shape who enters an opportunity.

Student access and opportunity

Mathematics, school-choice, and CCP work address different entry processes. New CCP interviews describe practical access barriers; the historical voucher analysis has a selective sample and does not establish outcomes under current eligibility rules.

Priority 6EmergingRA-064

Targeted placement evidence is relevant but not a statewide evaluation.

Automatic enrollment in advanced coursework

Wake County’s earlier threshold-based approach offers a related NC example. It should not be described as an evaluation of the later statewide policy.
