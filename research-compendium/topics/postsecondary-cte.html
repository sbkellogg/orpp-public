What we know—and what remains open

Priority 1EmergingRA-004

Eligibility alone does not ensure access.

Advanced learning access

CCP research adds delivery and participation evidence: advising, remaining costs, schedules, and transportation shape opportunities. These qualitative findings complement, rather than establish, effects of changing access rules.

Priority 1EmergingRA-005

Pathway benefits vary by model and outcome.

Postsecondary pathways

Early-college lottery evidence supports credential attainment. CCP analyses add favorable education and recorded-earnings associations, with selection and employment-data limits. They do not establish that one pathway or delivery format is best.

Priority 3EmergingRA-015

Implementation studies identify barriers that outcome averages can miss.

Implementation

LETRS observations, CCP interviews, and preliminary coaching reports describe how support and access work in practice. Such evidence helps define improvement questions but does not establish that a proposed change causes better outcomes. ATR annual case studies add district-specific evidence about roles and coaching time.

Priority 3EmergingRA-028

Delivery formats offer opportunities with different support needs.

Scheduling and delivery models

The NC dual-enrollment modality abstract reports favorable postsecondary outcomes across formats versus nonparticipation, while pass rates differ. It is not a randomized comparison of online and in-person provision.

Priority 3EmergingRA-038

Pathway models, course delivery, and access barriers are distinct.

Career and postsecondary pathways, including CTE modernization and innovative learning models

Early-college and CCP evidence spans credentials, employment, modality, and participation experiences. Related outputs from the same evaluation partnership should not be counted as independent replications.

Priority 6EmergingRA-056

Access policies can shape who enters an opportunity.

Student access and opportunity

Mathematics, school-choice, and CCP work address different entry processes. New CCP interviews describe practical access barriers; the historical voucher analysis has a selective sample and does not establish outcomes under current eligibility rules.

Research report2020 · NC-009

What Happens When You Combine High School and College? The Impact of Early Colleges on Postsecondary Performance and Completion

Julie A. Edmunds; Fatih Unlu; Jane Furey; Elizabeth Glennie; Nina Arshavsky

Lottery-offered students more often earned a postsecondary credential (44.3% versus 33.0%), particularly an associate degree. The overall bachelor’s completion difference at six years was not statistically significant.

Journal article2023 · NC-010

CTE-Focused Dual Enrollment: Participation and Outcomes

Julie A. Edmunds; Fatih Unlu; Brian Phillips; Christine Mulhern; Bryan C. Hutchins

About 9% participated. Participation was positively associated with college credits, high-school graduation, and college enrollment; participation disparities were smaller than in college-transfer dual enrollment.

The Role of Course Delivery Modality in Dual Enrollment
