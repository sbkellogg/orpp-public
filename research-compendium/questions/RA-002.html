Priority 1EmergingRA-002 · Supporting Question

Early learning pathways

What do longitudinal literacy and mathematics pathways reveal about when differences in achievement, placement, and risk first emerge?

Draft evidence synthesis

Early-learning findings depend on exposure and later context.

NC Pre-K studies use both participant and county-funding designs. The added middle-school follow-up reports sustained favorable funding associations; kindergarten findings remain more conditional. Shared cohorts and funding data limit independence across papers.

Based on NC-011, NC-012, NC-037.

Considerations for practice

Track several outcomes and distinguish funding exposure from enrollment.

What we still need to learn

Test recent cohorts and later outcomes.

Working paper2022 · NC-011

The Long-Term Effect of North Carolina’s Pre-Kindergarten Program Is Larger in School Districts with Lower Rates of Growth in Academic Achievement

Robert C. Carr; Tyler W. Watts; Jade M. Jenkins; Yu Bai; Ellen S. Peisner-Feinberg; Clara G. Muschkin; Helen F. Ladd; Kenneth A. Dodge

Estimated benefits of NC Pre-K funding were larger in districts with slower subsequent achievement growth. Funding benefits also added to the advantages of higher average district achievement.

Journal article2021 · NC-012

Effects of North Carolina’s Pre-Kindergarten Program at the End of Kindergarten: Contributions of School-Wide Quality

Robert C. Carr; Ellen S. Peisner-Feinberg; Rachel Kaplan; Irina L. Mokrova

Average-quality elementary schools showed no reliable overall NC Pre-K effect in this analysis. Language or working-memory benefits were evident under some higher-quality school conditions; literacy and mathematics moderation was not detected.
