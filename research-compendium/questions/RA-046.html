Priority 4EmergingRA-046 · Supporting Question

Resilience and recovery

Which school, district, workforce, and community conditions support stronger recovery following major disruptions?

Draft evidence synthesis

Recovery needs extend beyond test performance.

The indexed attendance and engagement work points to persistent barriers; tutoring offers a preliminary local intervention example. These sources cover selected pandemic-recovery questions.

Based on NC-001, NC-002, NC-022.

Considerations for practice

Coordinate engagement and instructional responses.

What we still need to learn

Evidence on disaster recovery and long-term resilience needs expansion.

Journal article2025 · NC-001

The Relationship Between Student Attendance and Achievement, Pre- and Post-COVID

Tom Swiderski; Sarah Crittenden Fuller; Kevin C. Bastian

More days absent were associated with lower mathematics achievement. The published analysis estimates that restoring pre-pandemic attendance could account for 13–19% of the academic recovery needed in 2023–24.

Journal article2025 · NC-002

In School, Engaged, on Track? The Effect of the Pandemic on Student Attendance, Course Grades, and Grade Retention in North Carolina

Sarah Crittenden Fuller; Tom Swiderski; Camille Mikkelsen; Kevin C. Bastian

Attendance, course grades, and grade progression worsened during the pandemic. Disruptions were uneven, with larger difficulties for some middle-grade and historically marginalized student groups.
