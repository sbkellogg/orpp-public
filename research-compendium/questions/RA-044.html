Priority 4EmergingRA-044 · Supporting Question

School climate

How are staff and student perceptions of leadership, safety, belonging, support, and climate associated with teacher retention, student attendance, academic growth, and differences in outcomes across student groups?

Draft evidence synthesis

Climate evidence spans measured associations and local accounts.

Working-conditions research and the community-school pilot provide different views of organizational and relational conditions. Neither establishes a universal climate intervention.

Based on NC-015, NC-025.

Considerations for practice

Use multiple perspectives when interpreting local climate.

What we still need to learn

Link student and staff measures to subsequent outcomes.

The Importance of Teaching and Learning Conditions: Influences on Teacher Retention and School Performance in North Carolina

Barnett Berry; Kevin C. Bastian; Linda Darling-Hammond; Tara Kini

Leadership, collaboration, planning time, community support, collective practice, and student conduct were positively associated with retention. Associations were often stronger in high-poverty schools.
