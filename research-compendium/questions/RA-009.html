Priority 2EmergingRA-009 · Supporting Question

Working conditions

How do school leadership, planning time and workload, educator voice, safety, professional learning, and instructional support relate to staff well-being, retention, and effectiveness; are there recurring combinations of conditions or thresholds associated with elevated risk?

Draft evidence synthesis

Workplace conditions are connected to retention.

An NC survey-and-interview study links organizational conditions to retention; rural project accounts add context. Neither establishes that a specific improvement will cause teachers to stay.

Based on NC-015, NC-023.

Considerations for practice

Use working-conditions evidence to identify and investigate local priorities.

What we still need to learn

Validate relationships using current measures and outcomes.

Research brief2021 · NC-015

The Importance of Teaching and Learning Conditions: Influences on Teacher Retention and School Performance in North Carolina

Barnett Berry; Kevin C. Bastian; Linda Darling-Hammond; Tara Kini

Leadership, collaboration, planning time, community support, collective practice, and student conduct were positively associated with retention. Associations were often stronger in high-poverty schools.
