Priority 4EmergingRA-042 · Supporting Question

Safety and discipline

How do school safety conditions, student conduct, educator perceptions of safety, and disciplinary practices relate to staff well-being and stability, student behavior, and academic outcomes?

Draft evidence synthesis

Discipline decisions can change without worsening average outcomes.

Early-grade reform and disparity studies address exclusion and fairness. A separate historical charter-entry study adds behavioral outcomes, but does not identify which discipline practices explain its estimates.

Based on NC-006, NC-007, NC-038.

Considerations for practice

Monitor both outcomes and fairness when reviewing discipline.

What we still need to learn

Replication, older grades, and implementation supports need attention.

Working paper2024 · NC-006

Ending Early Grade Suspensions

Ezra Karger; Sarah Komisarow

The reform reduced suspension probability by 1.4 percentage points and narrowed the Black–White suspension gap. Average test scores and disciplinary infractions did not worsen.

Journal article2022 · NC-007

Equal Time for Equal Crime? Racial Bias in School Discipline

Ying Shi; Maria Zhu

Comparisons within incidents and across incidents involving the same students found Black students faced higher suspension probabilities or longer suspensions than comparison students, consistent with racial bias.

Journal article2020 · NC-038

Social Returns to Private Choice? Effects of Charter Schools on Behavioral Outcomes, Arrests, and Civic Participation

Andrew McEachin; Douglas Lee Lauen; Sarah Crittenden Fuller; Rachel M. Perera

Students entering charter schools from traditional public schools had favorable absence, suspension, conviction, and voting estimates. Results were less consistent for students already in the charter sector.
