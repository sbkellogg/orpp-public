Priority 1EmergingRA-004 · Supporting Question

Advanced learning access

Who participates in advanced learning opportunities, including AIG, honors, AP/IB, advanced mathematics, CCP, advanced CTE, CDM, and related pathways, and where are the largest opportunity gaps?

Draft evidence synthesis

Access and success require attention at several stages.

The indexed work separates eligibility, nomination, participation, and persistence. Findings differ between elementary acceleration and CTE dual enrollment.

Based on NC-010, NC-013.

Considerations for practice

Check who is identified, who enrolls, and who remains engaged.

What we still need to learn

Comparable statewide evidence across advanced-learning pathways is still needed.

Studies and projects connected to this question

Working paper2022 · NC-010

CTE-Focused Dual Enrollment: Participation and Outcomes

Julie A. Edmunds; Fatih Unlu; Brian Phillips; Bryan C. Hutchins; Christine Mulhern

About 9% participated. Participation was positively associated with college credits, high-school graduation, and college enrollment; participation disparities were smaller than in college-transfer dual enrollment.

Journal article2020 · NC-013

Math Acceleration in Elementary School: Access and Effects on Student Outcomes

Steven W. Hemelt; Matthew A. Lenard

Girls were less likely to be nominated and qualify than similarly performing boys. Acceleration effects differed by gender, including evidence of short-term mathematics knowledge decay for girls.
