Priority 1EmergingRA-006 · Supporting Question

School-sector pathways

How do enrollment, mobility, access, and outcomes vary across school contexts, including charter schools and for populations such as Students with Disabilities?

Draft evidence synthesis

School-sector results differ across outcomes and entry decisions.

Charter research adds behavioral and civic outcomes for historical ninth-grade entrants. The early Opportunity Scholarship study reports positive estimates with strong volunteer-sample and test-comparability limits. Neither establishes current sector-wide superiority.

Based on NC-028, NC-036, NC-038.

Considerations for practice

Examine access, entry decisions, and variation within sectors.

What we still need to learn

Update representative outcomes for current eligibility and school contexts.

Studies and projects connected to this question

Journal article2017 · NC-028

The Growing Segmentation of the Charter School Sector in North Carolina

Helen F. Ladd; Charles T. Clotfelter; John B. Holbein

The study documented increasing sector segmentation and racial imbalance. For the historical period studied, charter schools were not more effective on average than traditional public schools.

Journal article2020 · NC-036

An Analysis of the Effects of North Carolina’s Opportunity Scholarship Program on Student Achievement

Anna J. Egalite; D. T. Stallings; Stephen R. Porter

New scholarship recipients had positive mathematics and language estimates, but no detectable reading effect. Positive estimates were concentrated in private schools using the same test family.

Journal article2020 · NC-038

Social Returns to Private Choice? Effects of Charter Schools on Behavioral Outcomes, Arrests, and Civic Participation

Andrew McEachin; Douglas Lee Lauen; Sarah Crittenden Fuller; Rachel M. Perera

Students entering charter schools from traditional public schools had favorable absence, suspension, conviction, and voting estimates. Results were less consistent for students already in the charter sector.
