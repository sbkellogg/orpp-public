Priority 1

Student Pathways, Access, and Outcomes

Follow evidence from early learning through advanced coursework and postsecondary transitions.

The agenda’s central question

How do students move through key academic and postsecondary pathways, where do differences in access and outcomes emerge, and which student, school, and system conditions are associated with successful transitions?

Priority 1EmergingRA-002

Early-learning findings depend on exposure and later context.

Early learning pathways

NC Pre-K studies use both participant and county-funding designs. The added middle-school follow-up reports sustained favorable funding associations; kindergarten findings remain more conditional. Shared cohorts and funding data limit independence across papers.

Priority 1EmergingRA-003

Systematic placement can open later mathematics pathways.

Middle-grades transitions

Wake County’s targeted middle-school acceleration study offers evidence about students near a test-score cutoff. It does not answer every question about grade 8 placement statewide.

Priority 1EmergingRA-004

Eligibility alone does not ensure access.

Advanced learning access

CCP research adds delivery and participation evidence: advising, remaining costs, schedules, and transportation shape opportunities. These qualitative findings complement, rather than establish, effects of changing access rules.

Priority 1EmergingRA-005

Pathway benefits vary by model and outcome.

Postsecondary pathways

Early-college lottery evidence supports credential attainment. CCP analyses add favorable education and recorded-earnings associations, with selection and employment-data limits. They do not establish that one pathway or delivery format is best.

Priority 1EmergingRA-006

School-sector results differ across outcomes and entry decisions.

School-sector pathways

Charter research adds behavioral and civic outcomes for historical ninth-grade entrants. The early Opportunity Scholarship study reports positive estimates with strong volunteer-sample and test-comparability limits. Neither establishes current sector-wide superiority.
